704. Binary Search

One of the simplest algorithms to explain.

And one of the easiest to mess up with `left` and `right`. 😄

The idea:

sorted array
     ↓
check middle
     ↓
discard half
     ↓
repeat

For:

nums = [-1,0,3,5,9,12]
target = 9

First:

left = 0
right = 5
mid = 2
nums[mid] = 3

`3 < 9`, so everything on the left can be ignored.

[ -1  0  3 | 5  9  12 ]
             ↑
           search

Now:

left = 3
right = 5
mid = 4
nums[mid] = 9

Found it → return `4`.

The important part isn't calculating `mid`.

It's knowing which half is impossible after every comparison:

if mid > target:
    right = m - 1
else:
    left = m + 1

That is what gives Binary Search its `O(log n)` complexity.

Instead of checking `n` elements:

n → n/2 → n/4 → n/8 → ...

For a missing target, the loop eventually reaches:

left > right

and we know there's nowhere left to search.

Time: O(log n)
Space: O(1)

The pattern to remember:

Sorted data + monotonic decision → think Binary Search.

The real skill isn't memorizing the template.

It's recognizing when you can safely throw away half the search space.
