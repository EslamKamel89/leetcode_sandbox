104. Maximum Depth of Binary Tree

Another tree problem where recursion does the heavy lifting.

The idea is simple:

For every node, ask:

What’s deeper — my left subtree or my right subtree?

Then:

depth = 1 + max(left_depth, right_depth)

For:

[3,9,20,null,null,15,7]

The DFS works from the bottom up:

9 → depth 1  
15 → depth 1  
7 → depth 1  
20 → 1 + max(1,1) = 2  
3 → 1 + max(1,2) = 3

Answer: 3

This is a classic post-order DFS pattern: calculate the children first, then use their results to calculate the parent.

Time: O(n)  
Space: O(h) for the recursion stack.

The reusable pattern:

When a tree node’s answer depends on its children, let recursion return the information the parent needs.

Sometimes recursion isn't magic. It's just delegation with a call stack.
