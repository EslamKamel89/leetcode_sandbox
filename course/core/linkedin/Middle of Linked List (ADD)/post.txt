876. Middle of the Linked List

Two pointers. One moves normally, the other is in a hurry. That’s basically the whole algorithm. 😄

For:

[1 → 2 → 3 → 4 → 5]

we start with:

slow = 1
fast = 1

Then:

slow → 1 step
fast → 2 steps

So the pointers move:

1, 1
2, 3
3, 5

When `fast` reaches the end:

while fast and fast.next:
    slow = slow.next
    fast = fast.next.next

`slow` is sitting exactly at the middle:

1 → 2 → [3] → 4 → 5
          ↑
        slow

For an even-length list:

1 → 2 → 3 → 4 → 5 → 6

`slow` ends at `4`, which is the second middle node.

Why does this work?

Because `fast` travels twice as quickly as `slow`.

When `fast` has traveled the entire list, `slow` has traveled roughly half of it.

No length calculation.
No extra array.
No second traversal.

Just two pointers doing their jobs.

Time: O(n)
Space: O(1)

The pattern to remember:

Fast moves 2 → Slow moves 1 → Fast reaches the end → Slow is in the middle.

A surprisingly useful trick for linked-list problems.
