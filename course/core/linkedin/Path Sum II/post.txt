113. Path Sum II

This is where DFS meets backtracking.

The goal isn't to find one valid path.

It's to find all root-to-leaf paths whose sum equals `targetSum`.

For:

targetSum = 22

5 → 4 → 11 → 2 = 22 ✅
5 → 8 → 4 → 5 = 22 ✅

The DFS carries two pieces of state:

path
curr_sum

At every node:

curr_sum += node.val
path += f"{node.val},"

When we reach a leaf, we check:

if not node.left and not node.right:
    if curr_sum == targetSum:
        res.append(...)

The important difference from a normal DFS:

We cannot stop after finding one answer.

We must continue exploring the remaining branches.

Think of the traversal as:

Explore
   ↓
Reach leaf
   ↓
Valid? → save path
   ↓
Backtrack
   ↓
Explore another branch

For example:

5 → 4 → 11 → 7
             ❌ 27

backtrack

5 → 4 → 11 → 2
             ✅ 22

Then DFS returns to `5` and explores the right subtree:

5 → 8 → 4 → 5
             ✅ 22

The key idea:

DFS finds the paths. Backtracking lets us explore all of them.

⏱️ Time: O(n) traversal
💾 Space: O(h) recursion depth, excluding the returned paths

This pattern shows up everywhere:

Choose → Explore → Check → Backtrack.

Once you recognize that pattern, a lot of recursive problems become much easier to reason about.
