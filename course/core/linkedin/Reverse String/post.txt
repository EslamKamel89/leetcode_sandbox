344. Reverse String

Sometimes the cleanest solution is just two pointers walking toward each other.

For:

`["h","e","l","l","o"]`

Start at both ends:

`left → h e l l o ← right`

Swap them:

`o e l l h`

Move inward and repeat:

`o l l e h`

Done.

The pattern is:

while left <= right:
    s[left], s[right] = s[right], s[left]
    left += 1
    right -= 1

No second array.
No reversed copy.
Just swap and move inward.

Time: O(n)
Space: O(1)

One small detail: `left <= right` means an odd-length string eventually swaps its middle character with itself. Completely harmless, although `left < right` would avoid that unnecessary final swap.

The reusable pattern:

Two ends → swap → move inward → stop when they meet.

Simple problem, but this two-pointer idea shows up in much less friendly disguises later.
